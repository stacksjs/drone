# Owned ground vehicle profile

Issue [#13](https://github.com/stacksjs/drone/issues/13) uses the shared owned vehicle runtime, raw IMU/GNSS acquisition and estimator, mission v2, authority and independent actuator watchdog. `createGroundProfile()` supplies a differential drive plant, controller, measured sensor renderer and simulated implement port. It has no physical actuator endpoint.

The plant is a 25kg planar two-wheel drive with passive support, 0.15m wheel radius, 0.6m track, 3kg m² yaw inertia, 0.08kg m² wheel inertia, and 3Nm maximum drive torque per wheel. Each wheel angular acceleration includes motor torque, contact reaction and viscous drag. Contact force follows slip velocity with a 160Ns/m stiffness and a Coulomb bound of half the normal weight times friction. Lateral slip has a 320Ns/m relaxation stiffness. Both wheels share the required lateral contact force; each longitudinal force is limited by the remaining Coulomb friction-circle budget. World velocity integrates the actual force vector, so turning does not rotate body velocity without lateral force. Body acceleration and yaw acceleration come from those contact forces, mass, inertia and drag. It does not teleport the body to a commanded velocity. Ground contact is a flat plane at origin down zero. Default friction is 0.65; the low-traction fixture uses 0.015. Neutral drive torque coasts if the separate spring brake is released. The fourth channel models normally engaged 4Nm brakes on each wheel; its neutral value is 1, so the independent watchdog engages brakes on lease loss. Brake torque dissipates angular momentum without driving the wheel backward. This is a modeled mechanical brake, not a hardware implementation.

The wheel geometry follows the differential-drive center/track convention documented by [WPILib](https://docs.wpilib.org/en/stable/docs/software/kinematics-and-odometry/differential-drive-kinematics.html). Our NED convention has positive clockwise yaw from north toward east, so a faster right wheel gives positive yaw. The equations and implementation are owned here; no external firmware or WPILib implementation is embedded.

The renderer quantizes cumulative wheel angle to signed 4096-count encoders. Count calibration identifies the fixed radius, track and encoder scale. It renders surface contact, stopped state, implement guard, emergency/bumper contacts and proximity ranges in millimetres. Each modeled packet carries capture epoch, sequence, estimator reference, 1ms declared clock uncertainty and receipt 40ms after capture. Qualification includes that 1ms in the 140ms upper age bound and accepts receipt latency only in 20..100ms. The runtime queues it until receipt. Ranges are synthetic fixture readings with a 20m clear sentinel, not a physical scanner or a complete occupancy map.

The decoder rejects stale packets over 140ms, future receipt, changed or replayed sequences, wrong calibration/reference, malformed counts/ranges/interlocks and unhealthy or stale owned localization. Identical retained packets may be reused while fresh. Calibrated wheel travel propagates local position using estimated yaw; a 20 percent correction to the owned GNSS position bounds wheel drift. Forward speed blends 80 percent GNSS and 20 percent wheel speed. A wheel/body forward-velocity discrepancy over 0.55m/s, or estimated lateral slip over 0.3m/s, for 200ms inhibits output. This is a modeled complementary odometry policy, not a production covariance estimator, certified traction monitor or GNSS-denied navigation solution. Initial yaw north is a simulator alignment assumption; absolute heading remains unobserved by the estimator.

The controller requests at most 0.65m/s, turns before driving when heading error exceeds 0.7rad, and slows approaching task targets. It consumes only qualified sensor packets and owned estimates. Its stopping envelope adds the 0.5m body radius, current measurement age, a further 100ms control/lease allowance, `v² / (2 * 0.5m/s²)`, three-sigma horizontal estimator uncertainty and 0.15m margin. The assumed 0.5m/s² braking bound is checked against the nominal modeled brake trajectory and requires physical validation. Obstacle detection inside this envelope or person detection inside the larger of this envelope and 2m latches inhibition. Opening the guard, losing surface contact, emergency input, timing gaps, traction loss or localization loss also inhibits. Recovery requires a new controller/session and explicit operator authority; fresh data does not clear the latch. Authority handles RC/manual takeover and independent host-loss leases. Inhibition sets zero drive torque, disables the mower and engages the modeled spring brake. The model can stop under its declared grip assumption; this does not prove a moving physical rover has stopped. Low grip can violate the nominal braking bound and triggers traction inhibition without claiming a guaranteed stop.

`planGroundCoverage()` plans up to three rectangular alternating strips, each 1..4m long with 0.5..1m swath. The default mission drives north 3m, shifts east 0.8m, then returns south along the second strip. Its two modeled 0.8m swaths cover a nominal 4.8m² rectangle; turns, imperfect tracking, vegetation and actual cut width require measured coverage evaluation. Obstacles stop the mission rather than create a detour. Arbitrary polygons, slopes, Ackermann steering and dynamic replanning remain open.

The mowing fixture requests a one-time acknowledged mower enable while stopped, drives the strips, requests disable, returns to the rally and stops. The logistics fixture drives to a depot, requests a scoped material drop from `tray-1`, returns and stops. The shared mission engine owns task acceptance, deadlines, energy budget, pause/resume, payload acknowledgement and failure handling. The simulated payload port checks qualified contact/proximity/guard data, stationarity, command location and event identity before confirming. A material tray is consumed only once, even if a caller supplies a new event ID. Mission waypoint arrival speed is limited to 0.08m/s before payload operations, below the measured stopped threshold. It does not command a blade, conveyor or gripper. Rejected commands are not retried automatically.

Scenarios are `nominal`, `logistics`, `low-traction`, `obstacle`, `people`, `manual`, `localization-loss`, `emergency`, `implement-open`, `sensor-loss` and `host-loss`. Fault fixtures activate at 4s. Proximity fixtures supply changed range readings; they do not simulate visual recognition or physical obstacle impacts.

The retained tests verify wheel inertia and yaw sign against worked equations, the independent Coulomb acceleration bound, calibrated raw counts, estimator correction, qualification and replay rejection, hazard latching, stopping-envelope arithmetic, payload interlocks, coverage geometry and snapshot isolation. Integrated runtime tests additionally exercise the common sensor-to-actuator path. These are modeled evidence. HIL and physical acceptance remain open: encoder/radius/track calibration under load, ground friction and slip detection across surfaces, braking distance on slopes and wet ground, measured sensor latency and blind regions, redundant emergency stop and RC control, blade coast-down/guard circuitry, payload retention/release sensing, position/heading qualification, actual coverage and stopping performance.
