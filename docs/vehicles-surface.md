# Owned surface vessel profile

Issue #14 uses the shared owned vehicle runtime, estimator, mission executor, authority lease and independent board gate. Each run allocates a fresh execution session with its own sensor sequence and sampler container ledger; reusing a profile does not reuse live execution state. `surface:simulate` exercises a modeled vector-thrust harbour vessel. No actuator or sampler hardware endpoints are enabled. Run `./buddy surface:simulate --scenario nominal --seconds 120 --survey storage/framework/runtime/harbour-survey.json` to export the modeled survey through native Stacks file storage. Export defaults declare 0.05 m horizontal position sigma, 0.001 rad attitude sigma and 2 ms clock sigma; explicit `--position-sigma`, `--attitude-sigma` and `--clock-sigma` options carry those assumptions into the retained inputs. These are synthetic example budgets, not measured sensor specifications.

The 30 kg planar hull has body surge and sway forces limited to 50 N and yaw moment to 12 Nm. Mass, yaw inertia 8 kg m², linear current-relative drag 30 N s/m and yaw drag 8 N m s are explicit in `surfaceHull`. Weak wind-relative drag is modeled at 0.2 N s/m. Four millisecond integration retains momentum; neutral outputs allow current and wind drift. There is no wave, roll, pitch, propulsion lag, saturation coupling, capsize or grounding dynamics model. The model needs a three degree of freedom vector-thrust actuator allocator; it does not represent every conventional twin-screw boat.

The controller consumes the owned raw-IMU/GNSS estimator and qualified rendered sensor packets, never hull truth. Position control runs at 20 ms, targets 0.7 m/s and uses measured yaw rate. The synthetic range sensor declares complete 360 degree coverage and 60 ms latency. Range includes the nearest circular harbour obstruction. The controller inflates it by hull radius, three range sigmas, reaction distance and a stop envelope assuming at least 0.5 m/s² deceleration. A collision, stale packet, replay, changed reference or scheduler gap latches inhibited output. This is an abort policy, not collision avoidance or COLREGs compliance. Zero thrust does not guarantee a physical stop.

The mission surveys two endpoints, explicitly requests one sample in `harbour-bottle-001`, returns to the declared safe point and settles below 0.25 m/s. Contact and stopped interlocks come from rendered measured quantities. Current and wind fixtures start after the 600 ms stationary estimator alignment. The `harbour-return` fixture inserts an explicit 100 percent energy decision after the first survey line. Consumed modeled energy selects the safe return branch and omits further survey and collection work. Nominal runs return after a successful sample. This deliberately strict authored threshold tests branch behavior; it is not an optimized energy policy. A sampler confirms only with enabled authority, a running payload task and an exact match to the pending event, task, operation, container, reference, issue/expiry epochs and requested position. Authority or task-scope loss during a pending sample latches uncertainty even if it later recovers. Uncertain or missing sample acknowledgement blocks the mission, requiring explicit operator inspection and a new run/container; it does not automatically repeat a collection or pretend the bottle is empty.

## Depth correction and export

The rendered sonar carries a quantized two-way travel time, 1480 m/s homogeneous sound speed, 0.3 m transducer draft and water level 0.4 m above `LOCAL_HARBOUR_MODEL_DATUM`. The bed fixture is 5 + 0.02 north + 0.01 east metres below that datum. `surfacePingFromMeasurement` preserves the capture/receipt epochs and reference. `correctSurfaceSounding` requires a capture-time pose from the same reference with an exactly aligned epoch, receipt latency at most 120 ms and a calibrated identity-mounted beam with capture-time attitude inside a 0.05 rad roll/pitch envelope. Slant range is travel time times sound speed divided by two. Vertical depth applies roll/pitch beam projection, adds draft and subtracts water level above the declared datum. The exported NED bottom position includes the attitude-projected horizontal acoustic footprint and transducer draft; the retained capture pose identifies vessel position separately. This local datum is synthetic and has no relationship to MLLW, LAT, geoid height or a real harbour benchmark.

Uncertainty is one standard deviation with independent travel-time, sound-speed, draft, water-level and residual-motion terms combined by root-sum-square. Vertical uncertainty includes first-order roll/pitch projection sensitivity. Horizontal uncertainty includes position, speed times clock uncertainty and a conservative independent three-axis angular bound. Caller-supplied position and attitude sigmas remain declared simulation assumptions, not estimator covariance: the owned estimator currently reports no attitude covariance. Unknown covariance, refraction, acoustic footprint, bottom classification, beam lever-arm uncertainty and angular correlations are not represented. Roll/pitch above the declared 0.05 rad envelope is rejected. The runtime propagates the last owned attitude to the measurement capture epoch using only a matching qualified BODY_FRD gyro with at most 60 ms extrapolation. Capture-pose provenance retains the original attitude and gyro epochs, original GNSS position epoch and both prediction epochs. Angular sigma must include uncertainty of that extrapolation; no full inertial fusion or absolute heading observation is claimed. Clock budgets must be finite and at least the sensor-declared sigma, which is bounded to 1..5 ms. The raw ping retains that declaration separately from the adopted clock budget so export revalidation rejects an understated budget. The export retains every raw correction input, timestamp, reference, position, local origin, datum, uncertainty convention and single-beam coverage limitation. It is a modeled JSON artifact, not an IHO S-102 product or certified charting survey.

NOAA describes sonar depth as a measured acoustic round trip and ties water-level reductions to a defined datum and benchmarks. Its survey requirements require instrument, sound speed, vessel motion, water level and other uncertainty sources to be evaluated for actual survey systems. These conventions motivate retaining raw corrections and explicit datum metadata here; our simplified budget does not demonstrate compliance with NOAA or IHO standards. Sources: [NOAA hydrographic equipment](https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/learn/hydrographic-survey-equipment.html), [NOAA standards and requirements](https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/publications/standards-and-requirements.html), [NOAA ellipsoidally referenced surveys](https://www.nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/learn/ellipsoidally-referenced-surveys.html).

## Remaining evidence

Physical gates remain: hull/allocator identification and stopping trials; waterproof power, RC takeover and independent kill; calibrated GNSS/IMU/DVL/compass and clock synchronization; surveyed sensor lever arms; sonar bar checks and sound-speed profiles; observed water-level datum transfer; uncertainty and coverage assessment; sample pump/valve/container identity, physical sealing and contamination controls; shore-supervised harbour trials and collision rules. Bench, HIL and water trials must be recorded separately. Simulated range and sample acknowledgements are not evidence of real sensing or real water collection.
