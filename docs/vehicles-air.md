# Owned fixed-wing profile

The `fixed-wing` v2 profile is our original aerodynamic trainer simulation on the common owned vehicle runtime. It starts motionless on a level runway, takes off using propeller thrust and elevator rotation, flies a survey waypoint, follows a descending approach and brakes after modeled wheel contact. All actuator outputs remain simulation-only. This is an executable development baseline for issue #12, not flight qualification.

`./buddy air:simulate --scenario nominal --seconds 120` runs the native Stacks command. `crosswind` adds a 2 m/s east wind after startup. `airspeed-loss`, `position-loss`, `host-loss`, `rc-override` and `kill` exercise distinct sensor or authority losses after 12 seconds. Fault inhibition means actuator leases cease or neutralize. An airborne aircraft can continue moving or fall after neutral output; these fixtures do not establish a safe emergency landing.

## Dynamics and control contract

The fictional airframe has mass 2 kg, wing area 0.8 square metres, span 2.4 m, chord 0.333 m and diagonal inertias 0.18, 0.25 and 0.35 kg square metres. Air density is fixed at 1.225 kg per cubic metre. Propeller thrust is bounded at 14 N and electrical power is modeled as 8 W plus 250 W times throttle. None of these constants describes a purchased or identified airframe.

The plant integrates Newton-Euler translation and rotation at a maximum 10 ms step. The common runtime uses 4 ms. Relative air velocity is ground velocity minus wind, rotated into BODY_FRD. Dynamic pressure is half density times speed squared. Lift acts perpendicular to the longitudinal relative airflow and drag opposes it. The pre-stall lift coefficient is 0.25 plus 4.8 times angle of attack, bounded before a declared post-stall loss. Drag includes profile, induced and incidence contributions. This follows the definitions in [NASA's lift equation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/lift-equation/) and [NASA's discussion of incidence and drag](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/inclination-effects-on-drag/). The actual polar and moment derivatives are original modeling assumptions, without wind-tunnel identification.

Aileron, elevator and rudder produce dynamic-pressure-scaled moments with angular-rate damping. Positive elevator in this API requests a nose-up moment; its sign is a declared actuator convention, not a wiring or servo configuration. Wheels contribute bounded rolling resistance and braking only during contact. Contact constrains the level runway vertically. The model does not resolve tire suspension, ground damage, propeller wash, surface actuator lag, propeller efficiency, aeroelasticity, spin, gust spectra, full sideslip-coupled lift or compressibility. A control surface has no aerodynamic authority at zero relative speed.

The controller receives owned estimator attitude and LOCAL_NED position and velocity, plus independent modeled pitot/angle/rate/contact observations. It never receives plant truth. A capture-bound packet reports quantized airspeed, angle of attack, sideslip, angular rates, wheel contact and a speed-threshold stopped indication. Its declared transport delay is 60 ms. Policy rejects future, stale, altered, lower-sequence and mismatched boot/reset-reference observations. Identical retained packets are allowed until their age limit of 160 ms. Sensor qualification and flight-envelope faults latch for the controller's lifetime. Restarting a controller does not authorize rearming.

Original bounded specific-energy feedback adjusts throttle using altitude and airspeed deficits. Pitch allocates climb rate while reducing nose-up demand as airspeed falls. Heading error sets a bank target bounded to 0.5 rad. The nominal target airspeed is 13 m/s; runway rotation begins at 10 m/s. The airborne envelope rejects speed below 8 m/s or above 25 m/s, excessive angle of attack, bank and pitch. This is a simulation envelope, not an aerodynamic certification or complete stall-recovery law. [FAA's flight-controls and aerodynamics reference](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/phak) explains why stall depends on critical angle of attack, not a single airspeed threshold.

Landing follows a 5.5% distance-based descent to the declared level runway, reduces speed to 10.5 m/s, then closes throttle and brakes after fresh contact. Mission completion additionally requires estimator arrival and qualified contact/stopped evidence in the common runtime. The land goal is 650 m north of startup, so the nominal route has room for departure and approach without an impossible zero-radius turn. A paused mission requests a bounded 45 m orbit instead of holding a stationary point. Physical and crosswind approach acceptance still require measured aircraft and runway evidence.

## Evidence and remaining scope

Public tests cover independent dynamic-pressure force values, lift loss after the declared stall angle, zero-speed surface authority, wind-relative lift, wheel kinetic-energy dissipation, controller energy response, packet replay and reference loss, malformed estimates, stall/bank inhibition, loiter decisions and caller ownership. Common runtime tests additionally exercise the raw IMU/GNSS pipeline through the owned estimator and mission engine. Those tests establish only the scenarios and bounds they execute.

The following issue #12 families remain explicitly unsupported: VTOL transitions and tilt mechanisms, helicopters, soaring and thermal exploitation, blimps and other buoyant aircraft, and spacecraft. Fixed-wing support here does not imply support for those families. Return and loiter guidance are available at the mission/control contract, but a complete wind-aware approach planner, stall recovery, airspeed sensor hardware calibration, identified coefficients, physical servo allocation, SITL comparisons and HIL/flight acceptance remain open. No external ArduPilot or PX4 control code is copied or required.
